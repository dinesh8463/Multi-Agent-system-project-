\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
When a rescue drone fails, the remaining drones must find out what happened and resume its work using incomplete information. We built a two-dimensional simulator in which drones search a map, detect survivors, and exchange observations over a changing radio graph. They use local bids or a distributed optimizer to propose assignments. Majority leases control who can execute a task, and heartbeat messages help the swarm release unfinished work after a failure.

We evaluated 29 conditions using 20 seeds per condition, for 580 recorded episodes. With 20\% scheduled drone failures, healing increased mission completion from 4 of 20 runs to 16 of 20. The strict recovery measure, which requires simultaneous reactivation of all the failed drone's unfinished tasks, was 96.25\% without healing and 93.75\% with healing. We investigated and retained this failed check. On a controlled graph, resilient consensus reduced estimation error under false reports. In the dynamic mission, distributed ADMM frequently fell back to the greedy auction. Severe radio degradation and the 100-drone runs exposed limits in finishing rescues despite high search coverage. These findings identify both where local coordination helped and where the design needs more work.

\textbf{Keywords} \quad multi-agent systems, task allocation, ADMM, resilient consensus, self-healing, signal temporal logic.
